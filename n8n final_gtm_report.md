# Market Research and GTM Plan

## 1. Executive Summary
Target small and mid-sized K–12 schools (and smaller postsecondary institutions) with an AI-first LMS that materially reduces teacher workload through automated grading, lesson planning, and differentiation while providing essential LMS capabilities, easy rostering/SSO, and clear privacy/compliance artifacts. Acquire through low-friction pilots and teacher-focused self-serve, and accelerate scale with partner channels (SIS/content/regional agencies). Early priorities: CSV rostering + SSO, OneRoster/LTI roadmap, pilot wins measured by teacher time-saved, and a scalable self-serve onboarding.

Key 12‑month outcomes
- Run 3–6 sponsored pilots → convert at least 1–2 to paid
- Demonstrate measured teacher time-saved per pilot (target 2–5 hours/week)
- Publish privacy whitepaper, DPA template, and SOC2 roadmap
- Deliver CSV rostering + SSO MVP and progress on LTI/OneRoster

## 2. Product Brief
Product (working name: [Product]) is an AI-enabled learning management platform designed for small and mid-sized K–12 schools and smaller postsecondary institutions. Core capabilities:
- AI-assisted automated grading for common item types (quizzes, short responses) with manual override and audit logs
- AI-assisted lesson planning: ready-to-use templates, standard alignment suggestions, and differentiation prompts
- Student differentiation suggestions and formative feedback drafts
- Core LMS features: assignments, gradebook, announcements, class pages
- Easy rostering: CSV import, simple SSO setup; OneRoster and LTI on roadmap
- Teacher-first UX with transparency controls (explainability notes, AI confidence, manual edit)
- ROI dashboard and pilot evaluation instrument to measure time-saved and grading turnaround
- Compliance artifacts: privacy whitepaper, DPA template, SOC2 roadmap
- Marketplace-ready for content partners and assessment integrations

Target minimum viable integrations
- CSV rostering and SSO (MVP)
- LTI / OneRoster integration (MVP roadmap)
- Content marketplaces and assessment partner connectors in Phase 2–3

## 3. Research Evidence Summary
Primary research and assumptions that inform the GTM:
- Teacher time-savings is the highest-motivating benefit. Early pilot hypothesis: 2–5 hours/week saved per teacher when grading and lesson-planning workflows are automated and templated.
- Small schools/colleges have short procurement cycles and limited IT bandwidth; they value predictable pricing and minimal-install solutions (CSV + SSO).
- Tech directors require clear privacy/compliance artifacts and minimal vendor IT burden (OneRoster/SSO, DPA, SOC2 intent).
- Pilots that include a measurable evaluation instrument, weekly/biweekly check-ins, and quick wins (first measurable time-saved within <14 days) convert at significantly higher rates.
- Channels with highest leverage: teacher communities (word-of-mouth), regional education service agencies, SIS resellers, and content partners.

Evidence types to collect in pilots
- Baseline and ongoing time-on-task logs (teacher-reported and system-captured)
- Grading turnaround times before/after
- Qualitative teacher interviews (30+ per pilot across roles)
- Adoption metrics (MAU/WAU teachers per school), NPS, and pilot satisfaction

## 4. Target Market
Primary market
- Small & mid-sized K–12 schools and groups (public single schools, private/parochial schools, small charters)
- Smaller postsecondary institutions (community colleges with decentralized IT)

Enrollment bands and decision characteristics
- Small: 200–800 students — rapid decision cycles, ideal pilot targets
- Mid: 800–3,500 students — require light AE support and sometimes regional approvals
- IT maturity: limited dev resources; preference for CSV import + SSO and OneRoster compatibility
- Budget sensitivity: prefer predictable per-school flat pricing or low per-student pricing; open to grant-funded pilots

Secondary market
- Regional service agencies, small district consortia, private school networks standardizing teacher tools

## 5. Customer Pain Points
- Excessive grading workload and lesson prep time for teachers
- Fragmented tools and manual rostering processes
- Lack of trust in opaque AI outputs and fear of losing instructional control
- Procurement friction for smaller schools and limited IT resources to integrate vendors
- Need for measurable evidence for grant-funded pilots and for communicating ROI to stakeholders

## 6. Competitor Analysis
Landscape overview
- Large incumbent LMSs and SIS vendors (e.g., Canvas, PowerSchool, Schoology/D2L, Brightspace) — broad feature sets but often heavyweight, slower to implement at the small-school level, and not AI-first.
- Free/lightweight classroom tools (Google Classroom, Microsoft Teams for Education) — low cost and widely used but limited automation for grading/lesson planning and limited privacy/compliance differentiation on AI features.
- Niche AI/assessment startups — focused point solutions (grading, adaptive practice) but lack a full LMS and rostering/SSO ease for small schools.
- Content marketplaces and assessment vendors (Khan Academy, NWEA, Renaissance) — partnership opportunities, sometimes bundled through SIS channels.

Differentiators vs incumbents
- AI-first workflows specifically designed to reduce teacher workload (grading + lesson planning + differentiation)
- Low-friction deployment (CSV import + SSO) and a pilot-driven evaluation model tailored to small schools
- Privacy/compliance-first messaging and concrete artifacts (DPA template, privacy whitepaper, SOC2 roadmap)
- Teacher-first controls: explainability, manual override, and audit logs

Key competitor risks
- Incumbents may bundle features or OEM AI into existing platforms
- Free platforms have strong penetration (e.g., Google Classroom) and low switching cost for teachers
- Niche AI startups may capture specific use-cases (grading or adaptive learning) but not the full LMS

Suggested competitive moves
- Emphasize measurable teacher time-saved rather than feature parity
- Target schools where incumbents are over-featured and painful to manage (single small schools, private/parochial)
- Build co-sell relationships with SIS and content vendors to lower procurement friction

## 7. Pricing Matrix
Pricing objectives: simple, predictable pricing that fits small budgets and supports grant/sponsored pilots; conversion from free teacher tier to paid school-level contracts.

Sample 3-tier pricing (suggested annual pricing; adjust for local market)
- Free / Teacher Tier (0 USD)
  - Audience: individual teachers / small pilot usage
  - Features: core LMS, CSV rostering, limited AI grading/lesson templates, community support
  - Conversion path: upgrade prompt after X students/classes or usage threshold; pilot offer to school admin

- Core School Tier (per-school flat or per-student hybrid)
  - Option A — Per-school flat (recommended for small schools): $2,000 / school / year (up to 800 students). Additional $1.50 per student above 800, cap at $6,000.
  - Option B — Per-student (for mid-sized): $6–8 / student / year (volume discounts apply)
  - Includes: full LMS + standard AI grading, lesson packs, SSO, CSV rostering, ROI dashboard, email support
  - Pilot conversion mechanism: pilot discount applied as credit toward the first year; conversion incentive if pilot meets success criteria (hours-saved target)

- Premium / District & Advanced Tier
  - $6,000+ / school / year or $12–15 / student / year for advanced features
  - Includes: advanced AI workflows, analytics & assessment integrations, priority support, white-glove onboarding, OneRoster/LTI configuration assistance, custom SLAs
  - Ideal for: mid-sized schools, small consortia, agencies

Pilot pricing and offers
- Sponsored pilot: 3–6 months heavily discounted or subsidized (e.g., $0–$500) with an evaluation instrument and conversion pathway (discount credit applied to first-year license)
- Pilot success criteria documented up-front (TTV <14 days, average hours-saved per teacher/week target met, adoption targets)
- Clear upgrade path and timeline for conversion (e.g., automatic proposal delivered after pilot close)

Packaging notes
- Per-school flat pricing reduces procurement friction for small schools and predictable budgeting for principals
- Per-student pricing may be preferable for mid-sized schools with more formal procurement processes
- Offer grant-friendly invoicing and pilot education/outcome documentation for foundations and grant officers

## 16. Risks and Mitigations
Risk: AI trust & accuracy concerns slow adoption
- Mitigations: Explainability features, audit logs, manual override; transparent onboarding examples; small-scale A/B validations and teacher testimonials.

Risk: Incumbent bundling and price competition
- Mitigations: Emphasize teacher productivity ROI and rapid TTV; focus on small schools where incumbents are over-featured; accelerate partner/distributor relationships.

Risk: Compliance & procurement burdens (FERPA/COPPA/state rules)
- Mitigations: Prioritize DPA, privacy whitepaper, SOC2 roadmap; provide procurement-ready templates; engage tech directors early to co-design contract terms.

Risk: Low willingness to pay in small schools
- Mitigations: Free teacher tier + sponsored pilots; pilot-to-value pricing and conversion credits; per-school flat pricing to simplify budgeting.

Risk: Integration friction / limited school IT capacity
- Mitigations: CSV rostering as initial path; minimal-install SSO options; partner with SIS vendors; provide white-glove onboarding for pilots.

Risk: Rapid regulatory change around AI
- Mitigations: Monitor regulatory landscape; adopt conservative data handling; include compliance counsel in contracts; design features to be configurable to meet new rules.

## 18. n8n vs CrewAI Comparison Notes
Context: selecting tooling for integrations and workflow automation during product and pilot rollout.

n8n (open-source automation/orchestration)
- What it is: Open-source workflow automation platform that connects APIs, performs ETL-like transformations, and orchestrates tasks.
- Strengths:
  - Rapid connector development for common SIS APIs and CSV processing
  - Low-cost (open-source) hosting option for MVP/early integration work
  - Good for scheduled jobs, data transformations, and routing events
  - Community of connectors and visibility into workflows
- Weaknesses:
  - Requires engineering resources to maintain and scale production-grade instances
  - Not inherently designed for AI orchestration or model explainability features
  - Operational overhead for secure hosting and scaling in education environments
- Best use case: MVP integrations (CSV/SSO ingestion, scheduled syncs with SIS, webhook processing) and proof-of-concept connectors during pilot phase

CrewAI (assumed: specialized AI workflow/orchestration platform)
- What it is: (If using an internal or third-party AI orchestration tool named CrewAI) a platform focused on coordinating AI prompts, models, and human-in-the-loop workflows.
- Strengths:
  - Purpose-built for AI orchestration and human review workflows (e.g., teacher review & override)
  - Potential for built-in explainability, audit trails, and model versioning
  - May offer turnkey connectors for common AI tasks and a productized interface for configuring workflows without deep engineering
- Weaknesses:
  - Potential higher cost or vendor lock-in
  - May lack breadth of non-AI connectors that n8n supports
  - Maturity and security posture should be validated for education deployments
- Best use case: Orchestrating AI grading pipelines, teacher-in-the-loop review steps, explainability UI, and audit logging for compliance-sensitive workflows

Recommendation
- Short term (pilot & MVP): Use n8n (or similar lightweight automation) for CSV ingestion, SSO handshakes, and initial SIS connectors to accelerate integration speed and reduce dev lead time. Use simple server-side orchestration to call AI services and return drafts to teachers.
- Medium term (post-pilot scale): Evaluate CrewAI (or a dedicated AI orchestration layer) to handle complex AI workflows, human-in-the-loop review, explainability, model versioning, and compliance logging. If CrewAI meets security/compliance requirements, adopt for production AI orchestration while keeping n8n for non-AI integrations or migrate integrations into a unified platform if operationally sensible.
- Always validate the vendor/security posture, SOC2 readiness, and data residency options before using third-party orchestration for student data.
